How your data is locked

Your data is encrypted with a random key. That key is stored only in a locked form that needs your passcode or passkey to open.

Read the details
  1. Your passcode or passkeyTyped or tapped, never stored
  2. Key derivationPBKDF2 · 600,000 rounds, or your authenticator’s PRF
  3. Wrapping keyOpens the data key, which exists unlocked only in memory
  4. Data keyRandom AES-256 key, stored only in wrapped form
  5. Your itemsEach encrypted with its own random IV

Questions

Can Google or the server read my codes?

No. Google only confirms your email address. Your data is encrypted in your browser with a key that needs your passcode or passkey, and the server only stores ciphertext. Read how it works.

What if someone has remote access to my computer?

While Vault is locked they can’t open it: nothing on the device can decrypt your data, and a passkey needs your fingerprint, face or PIN. If they’re controlling the computer while you have it unlocked, they can see what you can see, which is why short auto-lock times matter. Set Auto-lock to “When I leave this tab”.

Why do I need to enter my passcode every time?

That’s the point. Anything that unlocks Vault automatically could be used by someone else on your computer. Use a passkey if you want unlocking to be quick without being less safe.

What if I forget my passcode?

With a Google account, use your recovery key. With no account there is no recovery, so keep an exported backup and consider adding a passkey as a second way in.

Can I move my codes from another app?

Yes. Scan the export QR code from Google Authenticator, or import a file of otpauth:// links, from Settings.
