How Vault protects your data

Vault is built so that your data never leaves your device unencrypted. Here is exactly what it does, and what it can't do.

Encryption

Each item is encrypted with AES-256-GCM, which also detects tampering. The key comes from your password through two chained key-derivation steps: PBKDF2 with SHA-512 (500,000 iterations), a keyed HMAC step, then PBKDF2 with SHA-256 (another 500,000). Every encryption uses a fresh random salt and IV.

Your password is never stored. Vault keeps only a verifier built the same way, which can confirm a password but can't recover it.

Where data lives

Vaults are saved in your browser's local storage, already encrypted. Vault has no accounts and no server. Because of that, clearing your browser data deletes your vaults. Export a backup from Settings and keep it somewhere safe.

Automatic locking

  • 5 minutes without activity
  • 2 minutes with the tab in the background
  • Immediately when you leave or close the page

Locking clears the password, decrypted items and previews from memory. This is also why the open vault is a single page: moving between pages locks it.

Passkeys

A passkey lets you unlock with your fingerprint, Face ID or device PIN. Passkeys are tied to this device and need the site to be served over HTTPS. A vault with a password can also be shared by link; a passkey-only vault can't.

Extra passcodes

Any note or file can have its own passcode. It is combined with your vault password, so opening that item needs both.

Sharing

A share link contains an encrypted copy of the vault in the part of the URL after the #, which browsers never send to a server. Anyone with the link still needs your password. Treat the link as sensitive anyway, since it carries your encrypted data.

Limits to know about

Vault can't protect a device that already has malware, and a weak password is the weakest link. Choose a long, unique password. There is no recovery option: if you forget the password and have no passkey or backup, the data can't be opened.

Vault runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
